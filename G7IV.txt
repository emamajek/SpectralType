  4 Keenan classifications, all different - settled on G7IV Hdel1.
  This is a very interesting star, and probably a refugee from the
  inner galaxy.  Remarkably metal-rich ([Fe/H]=+0.38;Soubiran22) and
  old (8.9+-1.3Gyr;Metcalfe26/asteroseismo), evolving off MS.  See
  detailed study by Metcalfe26(2026AJ....171..287M). How the hell was
  the galaxy producing [Fe/H]=+0.38 stars 9 Gyr ago?  Current orbit
  U,V,W = +116, -31, -19 km/s, Rmin=5.15kpc, Rmax=11.08kpc
  (Trevisan11), Andersen12 estimates Rmin=4.307kpc,
  Rmax=12.066kpc. Nepal24 finds that super-metal-rich stars
  ([Fe/H]~+0.4) in solar neighborhood "are predominantly old (9-12
  Gyr)" that likely formed in the inner galaxy and were migrated to
  orbits that brought them 'out here' during the formation of the bar
  ~3-4 Gyr ago.  Taking the xHIP catalog, if one selects metal-rich
  [Fe/H]>0.3 Hipparcos stars within 100 pc, the one with the highest
  UVW velocity (heliocentric Stot=122.0km/s) is HIP 95447 (31 Aql),
  the one with the lowest U (U=-116.4km/s), i.e. highest velocity
  radially away from Galactic center.  V=5.17(HIP),
  B-V=0.761+-0.018(HIP),
  V-I=0.75+-0.07(HIP). plx=67.0153+-0.0657mas(GaiaEDR3) => Mv=4.30.
  Soubiran22/PASTEL mean values: Teff=5593+-19K(N=35),
  logg=4.19+-0.03(N=26), [Fe/H]=+0.38+-0.01(N=29).
  Prot=41d(Baliunas96), Metcalfe26 measured 5 periods from archival
  Mt. Wilson data: Prot=39.1+-0.6d(Metcalfe26/MW/1983/Nobs=69),
  Prot=41.8+-0.4d(Metcalfe26/MW/1984/Nobs=75),
  Prot=44.1+-0.5d(Metcalfe26/MW/1992/Nobs=36),
  Prot=41.1+-3.0d(Metcalfe26/MW/1994/Nobs=45),
  Prot=34.9+-0.5d(Metcalfe26/MW/2000/Nobs=25) => Metcalfe26 quotes
  mean Prot=40.3+-1.5d.  Also, Fulton(priv.comm.) measured Prot=36.33d
  during 1 season of archival NEID data. logR'HK=-5.099(Baliunas96).
  Metcalfe26: "We combine asteroseismology from the Transiting
  Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS) with spectropolarimetry from the
  Large Binocular Telescope (LBT) to test these predictions in the old
  metal-rich subgiant 31 Aql. The LBT observations reveal a strong
  large-scale magnetic field in this star, and archival measurements
  of its chromospheric emission over 50 yr confirm that it is
  noncycling, as predicted. The star exhibits a variety of rotation
  periods during different observing seasons, consistent with DR but
  with no means of distinguishing between solar-like and antisolar
  patterns. We incorporate the TESS observations to estimate the
  current wind-braking torque of 31 Aql, demonstrating that it
  supports revived magnetic braking in this old subgiant."
